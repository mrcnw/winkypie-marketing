# Brief — WP_P2_STATIC_swipingback

**Single variable tested:** **the profile, not the photograph.** Every other creative in the
set argues about one image — the first photo, the check, the grid. This one simulates the
thing the images are *for*: a dating profile, card after card, the same man in four settings
with the distance ticking down. The claim is that a man does not buy a photo, he buys a
profile that works.

**Evidence:** the owner worked from an Instagram post —
`https://www.instagram.com/p/DdRWxHFMX6h/`, recorded in the frontmatter as `reference`.
**It has not been read.** Instagram serves post pages behind a login wall, and the attempt on
2026-09-22 returned nothing. So no mechanism has been extracted from it, nothing in this brief
is derived from it, and `modelled_on` stays empty rather than pointing at a swipe entry that
does not exist. If it is worth keeping, it needs a pass with [[02 How To Find A Good Ad]] and
an entry in `good-ads.json` — until then it is a pointer, not evidence.

## The superseded cut — `v0-drifted`

The earlier 13.4 s version was renamed on 2026-09-22 rather than deleted, because the reason
it lost is worth keeping. It ended on two paraphrases of the same locked line — *"Get pro
authentic photos and more matches."* and *"One selfie. Real photos. More matches."* — where
PRODUCT.md §1 says those lines are identical everywhere and are not to be drifted. It also
carried no §11.2 strip, and closed on "Get noticed on dating apps." instead of "Be the right
swipe."

Drift is the failure mode this format invites: four plates and four closers is eight lines,
and writing eight lines is how a locked one gets improved by accident.

## The blocker

> **Is nobody swiping back?**

The opening plate asks the viewer about **his own dating results**. PRODUCT.md §11 gives this
exact shape as the worked example of what Meta rejects — *"Still single?"*, *"Tired of being
ignored?"* — and the root `CLAUDE.md` guardrail 8 says never second-person implications about
the viewer's romantic life.

**It is one frame of seven and nothing downstream depends on it.** The rest of the piece is
about photographs and about the app. A replacement plate that keeps the energy without the
accusation, in the register the other creatives use:

> **A profile is not one photo.**

Same length, lands in the same beat, and it sets up card two instead of interrogating the
viewer. Recommended, and it is a re-render of one plate rather than a re-cut.

Unfixed, this creative should not go up: it is the one violation in the set that is a decision
nobody has argued for, unlike campaign 9's headline, which the owner took deliberately with a
compliant twin standing by.

## Two ads, one creative

Two ads in the same ad set, same cut, different copy. The variable is **the promise against
the objection**: A leads with what he gets, B leads with the thing that stops him buying.

**Why the objection is worth one of the two slots.** PRODUCT.md §3 lists his buying blockers
and two of the three are the same worry — *"thinks AI photos look fake"* and *"unsure it will
still look like him"*. Every creative in this round answers that worry somewhere in the middle
and **not one of them opens with it.** They all sell the outcome and reassure afterwards. So
the set has never tested whether leading with the objection outperforms leading with the
promise, and that is a question whose answer changes every ad we write after it.

§5 supplies the honest answer to use: *"not a head pasted on a stock body"* — the
selfie-mirroring workflow, differentiator 2, which is also what beat 6 of this cut puts on
screen. The objection is not being handled with an adjective, it is being handled with the
mechanism.

**This pair tests a message, not a sentence — so the headline moves with the primary text.**
That is a different kind of test from [[WP_P2_STATIC_checkcard]], where the headline is held
identical and only the opening sentence changes. Do not read the two results as the same sort
of evidence: checkcard isolates a sentence, this isolates an argument. Description and call to
action are held constant in both.

## Primary text — A · the set

> A profile is not one photo, it is a set. WinkyPie turns one selfie into that set —
> different places, different light, and the same face and build in every frame. No
> photographer, no shoot day.

Headline: **A profile is not one photo.** ·
Description: **One selfie. No photographer.**

27 characters, and it is this variant's own opening sentence — the argument in five words.

**It is also the plate recommended for beat 1**, which is the point. If that frame gets
re-rendered as proposed, the ad's link card and the creative's first second say the same
thing, and the piece stops opening with a question about the viewer and starts opening with a
claim about profiles. Headline and hook converging is worth more here than variety between
them.

## Primary text — B · the objection

> The usual problem with AI photos is the face. It comes back close, not exact — a head on
> somebody else's body. WinkyPie works the other way round: you match the pose yourself, so
> the composition is real and the man in the photo is the man who took the selfie. One selfie
> in, a set for a dating profile back.

Headline: **Same face. Same build.** ·
Description: **One selfie. No photographer.**

The headline is beat 5's own closer, so the ad and the cut say one thing — and beat 5 is the
frame that proves it, with the source selfie beside the results and the §11.2 strip under
them. This is the only creative in the set whose footage can back an authenticity-led opener,
which is why the test belongs here and not on a card.

**"A head on somebody else's body" is ours, not an insult invented for the ad** — it is §5's
own wording for differentiator 2, and the whole point is that it describes a real failure mode
we avoid rather than a rival we name. Same register as campaign 9's "Most AI photo apps charge
you for the bad result": a category, never a company, and never a third-party mark.

**Nothing here claims the photos are not AI.** §11.5 requires honesty about that, and the copy
keeps it: it says the face and the build are his and that the composition is real, which is
what selfie-mirroring actually delivers. It does not say "real photos" or "not AI".

If B loses, the objection is quieter than §3 makes it look and the set should keep selling the
outcome. If B wins, every creative after this one opens differently.

One failure, and it is the plate above. The check stays red until that frame is replaced.

## Production flags

**None.** The cut exists, the subject is generated and the pose capture is real. The open item
is a single plate to re-render, which is listed as the blocker rather than as production.

## Ratios

**9:16 only, and it is the right one.** This is the only creative in the set born vertical: the
profile cards, the selfie and the pose catalog are all full-height. It is native to Reels and
Stories, and in the feed Meta letterboxes or centre-crops it — the plates and the cards sit on
the middle axis, so both are survivable. A 4:5 would be a re-layout, not a crop, and is only
worth it if this earns a second round.
