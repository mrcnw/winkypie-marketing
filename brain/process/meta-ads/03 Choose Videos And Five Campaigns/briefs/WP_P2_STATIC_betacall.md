# Brief — WP_P2_STATIC_betacall (candidate)

**Only as a real programme.** Roast's "We are looking for 30 guys who open Tinder and get
zero matches" has run 107 days ([[Winning Ads 2026-09-02]] #7) because a casting call is
scarcity without a fake countdown. [[Hooks And Angles]] dropped it for us on 2026-09-02
because we could not fulfil it. It comes back only if we **actually** give 30 men a free
month, collect their feedback and get permission to quote them — at which point it is
honest, and it also manufactures the one thing this account lacks: real user stories
([[Hooks And Angles]] says the fake-friend and "724,000 guys" patterns unlock only with
real users). It is not in round one; it is the first candidate for wave two.

## Why the format works (mechanism, not imitation)

Low-fi static — a phone screenshot or a printed note — is the niche's longest-living look
(Roast's top-impression ads, [[Competitor Landscape]]). The casting-call frame turns "buy an
app" into "apply to a thing", which is a smaller ask and a real filter: applicants
self-select as men with a photo problem, without the ad ever saying so about the viewer.

## Hook (verbatim — third person about the programme, never about the viewer)

> We're looking for 30 men to test a new dating-photo app.

Second beat, smaller: "Free month. Honest feedback. Pick a pose, take one selfie, tell us
what you got." Footer: App Store badge + "Apply in the app" or a short form link.

**Banned wording:** "who get zero matches", "if you can't get a match", any age bracket —
personal-attributes territory that Roast pays for and we do not (PRODUCT.md §11). The ad
describes the programme; it never diagnoses the viewer.

| Quoting them later | Written permission in the questionnaire; any later ad using their words carries the FTC material-connection disclosure (free access) and they are bona fide users by construction | $0 |

## Cost scenarios for the ad itself

Production: **$0** — designed in-house in about an hour; a screenshot of the real
photo-check "pass" screen with the two lines over it, or the printed-note look. Media, run
inside the same ad set as everything else:

| Scenario | What it proves | Media spend `[CPM and CPI unsourced — replace with round-one numbers]` |
|---|---|---|
| Read the hook only | CTR at the 3,000-impression floor | $25–45 at a $8–15 CPM |
| Read installs | 20 installs at a $4–8 CPI | $80–160 |
| Fill the programme | 30 applicants at a form-completion rate we do not know yet | `[unknown]` — this is the number the first week teaches |

**All-in for a first run: ≈ $100–200 of media, ≈ 9 hours of our time, plus the generation
compute of 30 free users.** Compared with the $500 Billo package it is the cheapest
hypothesis in the set — and the only one that produces round-two material as a by-product.

## Not yet decided

  ad. Owner to say yes.
- Offer-code mechanics and quotas in App Store Connect — verify before promising anything.
- Whether "Apply in the app" or an external form — the form filters better, the app installs
  faster.

## Do this, in order

   permission to quote. No decision, no ad.
2. Verify subscription offer codes and their quotas in App Store Connect before anything is
   promised; write the mechanics into this brief.
3. Write the application form (age band 22–40, iPhone, one sentence on his photos) and the
   day-7 and day-30 questionnaires, with the permission-to-quote line.
4. Only then design the static: a screenshot of the real Photo Check pass screen, or the
   printed-note look, with the two lines over it. No age callout, no "matches" on frame.
5. Export 9:16, 4:5 and 1:1; name `WP_P2_STATIC_betacall_<ratio>_v1` per [[Creative Naming]].
6. Run it in the same ad set as everything else; pause it the day the 30 seats fill — the
   scarcity is honest only while it is true.
