# Brief — WP_P2_STATIC_coworker

**Single variable tested:** whether **peer discovery** sells better than mechanism. Every other
creative in the set explains what the product does; this one says somebody else got there
first and you are behind. The claim is social, not technical, and P2 — the grinder who has
optimised everything else — is the persona it bites.

**Approved by the owner on 2026-09-22 and running as `v1`, carrying four §11 failures on
purpose.** They are listed in *What is being carried* below and recorded as `waived:` rather
than `approved:` — the preflight will show them red for as long as they are on the frame,
which is the point of that state.

**Evidence:** [[roast-same-face-10x]] — `2014738899400393`, named by the owner as the model.
It was **#1 on Roast's page by impressions** and ran 2026-04-24 → 2026-09-14 before stopping:
143 days, in a niche where a static living past 100 days is the signal we look for. The "Same
face" family it belongs to is the most-cloned thing on that page, roughly 257 ads. This is the
best-evidenced angle we have modelled on anything.

## What the model actually does — and where it keeps its risk

Its primary text is the whole ad:

> *"His coworker joined Hinge the same week. He had 20 matches by Friday. He had 2. Turned out
> he'd been using Roast. Did the same thing. This is his phone one week later. He was
> gatekeeping this the whole time."*

The creative is a phone screenshot of a full match list. The link card reads **"Same face. 10x
the matches."**

**Read where each piece lives.** The story is in the primary text. The multiplier is in the
headline. The proof is one screenshot. All three of those are fields Roast can edit in the ad
manager in thirty seconds — so when a rejection lands, it costs them an edit. Nothing risky is
baked into a file.

That is the transferable lesson from this ad, and it is not the story: **keep the claims that
can get you rejected in the fields you can edit.**

## Hook (verbatim, must be legible in 1.5 s, sound off)

> My coworker's profile photos looked like a photographer took them.

Silent and text-led, so the hook is the first plate rather than a spoken line. The piece runs
without audio by design: nothing is said, so there is no read to caption, no voice to record
and no bed to duck.

## What is being carried

`v1` reproduces its model faithfully enough to inherit four things our own rules exclude, and
it burns all of them into a rendered file. The owner's decision is to run it anyway.

| In the render | The rule |
|---|---|
| Closing plate: **"WinkyPie · x10 the matches."** | An invented multiplier with no source. Our own swipe note for this exact ad says "the '10x' multiplier is an invented stat we never copy", root `CLAUDE.md` lists "10x your matches" among the words that may not be used, and §8 requires every percentage to trace to a citable source |
| **"My coworker's profile photos looked like a photographer took them…"** | A fabricated testimonial. §11.1 — no invented testimonials, user counts or ratings, not even as placeholder. The FTC and the EU UCPD both enforce this, and a made-up person is the clearest version of it |
| **A scroll of Tinder match and message notifications** | Invented proof twice over, and read frame by frame on 2026-09-22 it is sharper than "a dating-app interface": **Tinder's wordmark and flame logo are on frame, repeated on every one of a dozen notification cards**, beside invented names and invented messages. That is a third party's trademark used in our paid advertising, on top of the fabricated proof. [[WP_P2_STATIC_checkcard]] draws the line — "no dating-app interface anywhere; ours runs the product we own" |
| All of the above **rendered into an mp4** | The model's lesson, inverted. Roast's risk sits in editable fields; ours sits in a file |

**Recorded as `waived:`, not `approved:`.** Two of the four — the unsourced multiplier and the
fabricated match list — are not risks being weighed, they are statements presented to a viewer
as true. Marking them approved would put that claim in the data; waiving them records the
decision without it. The guardrail check therefore stays red on the card for as long as those
elements are on the frame.

**If review sends it back, change the file, not the fields.** The model keeps its story and
its multiplier in the primary text and the headline, where a rejection costs an edit. The next
cut should do the same: strip the closing plate and the notification scroll out of the render
and let the text fields carry whatever is left.

## What the next cut looks like

The mechanism worth keeping is **"proof shown, not claimed"**. For Roast the proof is a match
list. **For us the proof is the photographs**, because photographs are what we sell — which
means the version of this ad that needs no waiver shows a set of results, and that is already
[[WP_P2_STATIC_oneselfie]].

So what this campaign adds on top of campaign 11 is the **story frame**. Three rules for the
cut that drops the waivers:

1. **No named or implied individual.** Not "my coworker", not "a guy I know". The moment a
   person is described, it is a testimonial.
2. **No number.** Not 10x, not 20 matches, not "double". §8 or silence.
3. **No dating-app interface.** The result photos carry the proof, on our own surfaces.

What survives is the observation, which is true and belongs to nobody:

> The guy whose photos look professional usually did not hire anybody.

Second person stays off his dating results and on the photographs, the same line campaigns 10
and 11 hold.

## Primary text (Meta placement)

> some dating profiles look like every photo came from a shoot. almost none of them did. you
> pick a pose, match it with a selfie, and WinkyPie renders you into it. same face, same
> build, one selfie in. nobody puts that part in their bio.

Headline: **Authentic pro photos. More matches.** ·
Description: **One selfie. No photographer.**

**Lowercase on purpose, and it is the variable.** Everything is lower case except the brand
name — one capitalised word in a field of small letters is the only thing that cannot be read
as a typo, and it pulls the eye exactly where it should go. `BRAND.md`'s sentence-case rule
governs headlines on frame, not the Meta text field, so nothing is being broken here. But it
is a register decision rather than a copy decision: **one creative in lower case beside four
in sentence case reads as carelessness, not as a voice.** It is deliberately confined to this
campaign, which is already the most reckless thing in the set. If it works, it becomes the
house register for Meta primary text; if nobody notices, it comes back.

**Written against the model's own copy**, read across five variants of it on 2026-09-22:

> *"we joined the same week. same city, same age, same apps. he had 20 matches by friday and i
> had 2, and he'd been using roast the whole time. 724,000+ guys already did it, the free quiz
> takes 2 min at roast.dating."*

> *"zero matches for six weeks. fixed my photos on sunday, first match tuesday morning. same
> face, same bio. 724,000+ guys already did it, the free quiz takes 2 min at roast.dating."*

**What that family actually is:** one creative with four primary texts, where **only the
opening story rotates** and the last sentence is identical in every one. Which is the same
machine as `## Primary text — A / B` here, run at four.

**And the correction worth recording.** An earlier read of a single variant concluded the
mechanism was the third person. Across five it is mostly the **first** — "i had 2", "my
brother and i", "fixed my photos". The rule is not first against third, it is **never *you***:
Meta prohibits claims about the *reader*, so a sentence about the speaker is safe in any
person. That distinction is what makes their copy sound like an accusation while clearing the
policy, and it is the part worth copying.

What we take from it is the grammar — flat declaratives, no adjectives, mundane specificity,
a reveal in the middle, and a closer that implies something was being withheld. Our last
sentence does the job their "he was gatekeeping this the whole time" does, without inventing
the man who was doing the gatekeeping.

Ours keeps all of that: seven beats, hard stops, the reveal in the middle ("pick a pose, match
it with a selfie"), the closer with the same withheld-secret energy ("nobody puts that part in
their bio" against "he was gatekeeping this the whole time").

**Two things are deliberately not taken.**

| In the model | Why it stays out |
|---|---|
| **"20 matches by Friday. He had 2."** | Invented figures. §8 requires every number to trace to a citable source, and this creative already carries one unsourced multiplier as a waiver. Adding two more in the field we can edit would be spending the waiver twice |
| **"724,000+ guys already did it"** | In the fixed closer of **every** variant of theirs, so it is not a detail to strip — it is the load-bearing sentence. An invented user count is §11.1, the guardrail that is about law rather than taste, and it stays out whole |
| **The coworker himself** | A fabricated person is a fabricated testimonial (§11.1). The frame already carries one and the owner waived it; putting a second one in the primary text would double the exposure rather than move it. Ours makes the same point about a **category of profiles**, which is a claim nobody has to have made |

**If the literal version is wanted** — a named-less man, a before-and-after count, the
gatekeeping line — it is writable, and it would need its own line in *What is being carried*.
It is a decision, not a rewrite, and it is not one this brief takes on its own.

## Ratios — decided 2026-09-22

**4:5 only, placements open, crop accepted.** Meta does not reject a mismatched ratio: in
Stories and Reels it applies *Smart Zoom*, cropping roughly 15 % off each side, or it
letterboxes — and the advertiser does not choose which.

This piece takes it. Both text plates and the closing lock-up are centred on the middle axis
and survive either. What a Smart Zoom trims is the outer edge of the notification cards — the
app label on the left, the timestamp on the right — which costs detail, not argument.

A purpose-built 9:16 would still be the cheapest in the set to make, because the content is a
vertical scroll already: it needs restacking, not re-composing. Worth doing if this creative
earns a second round, and it goes in through per-placement asset customisation rather than as
a second ad.
