# Brief — WP_P2_STATIC_coworker

**Single variable tested:** whether **peer discovery** sells better than mechanism. Every other
creative in the set explains what the product does; this one says somebody else got there
first and you are behind. The claim is social, not technical, and P2 — the grinder who has
optimised everything else — is the persona it bites.

**Approved by the owner on 2026-09-22 and running as `v1`, carrying four §11 failures on
purpose.** They are listed in *What is being carried* below and recorded as `waived:` rather
than `approved:` — the preflight will show them red for as long as they are on the frame,
which is the point of that state.

**Evidence:** [[roast-same-face-10x]] — `2014738899400393`, named by the owner as the model.
It was **#1 on Roast's page by impressions** and ran 2026-04-24 → 2026-09-14 before stopping:
143 days, in a niche where a static living past 100 days is the signal we look for. The "Same
face" family it belongs to is the most-cloned thing on that page, roughly 257 ads. This is the
best-evidenced angle we have modelled on anything.

## What the model actually does — and where it keeps its risk

Its primary text is the whole ad:

> *"His coworker joined Hinge the same week. He had 20 matches by Friday. He had 2. Turned out
> he'd been using Roast. Did the same thing. This is his phone one week later. He was
> gatekeeping this the whole time."*

The creative is a phone screenshot of a full match list. The link card reads **"Same face. 10x
the matches."**

**Read where each piece lives.** The story is in the primary text. The multiplier is in the
headline. The proof is one screenshot. All three of those are fields Roast can edit in the ad
manager in thirty seconds — so when a rejection lands, it costs them an edit. Nothing risky is
baked into a file.

That is the transferable lesson from this ad, and it is not the story: **keep the claims that
can get you rejected in the fields you can edit.**

## Hook (verbatim, must be legible in 1.5 s, sound off)

> My coworker's profile photos looked like a photographer took them.

Silent and text-led, so the hook is the first plate rather than a spoken line. The piece runs
without audio by design: nothing is said, so there is no read to caption, no voice to record
and no bed to duck.

**Recorded as `waived:`, not `approved:`.** Two of the four — the unsourced multiplier and the
fabricated match list — are not risks being weighed, they are statements presented to a viewer
as true. Marking them approved would put that claim in the data; waiving them records the
decision without it. The guardrail check therefore stays red on the card for as long as those
elements are on the frame.

**If review sends it back, change the file, not the fields.** The model keeps its story and
its multiplier in the primary text and the headline, where a rejection costs an edit. The next
cut should do the same: strip the closing plate and the notification scroll out of the render
and let the text fields carry whatever is left.

## What the next cut looks like

The mechanism worth keeping is **"proof shown, not claimed"**. For Roast the proof is a match
list. **For us the proof is the photographs**, because photographs are what we sell — which
means the version of this ad that needs no waiver shows a set of results, and that is already
[[WP_P2_STATIC_oneselfie]].

So what this campaign adds on top of campaign 11 is the **story frame**. Three rules for the
cut that drops the waivers:

1. **No named or implied individual.** Not "my coworker", not "a guy I know". The moment a
   person is described, it is a testimonial.
2. **No number.** Not 10x, not 20 matches, not "double". §8 or silence.
3. **No dating-app interface.** The result photos carry the proof, on our own surfaces.

What survives is the observation, which is true and belongs to nobody:

> The guy whose photos look professional usually did not hire anybody.

Second person stays off his dating results and on the photographs, the same line campaigns 10
and 11 hold.

## Primary text (Meta placement)

> The men whose dating photos look professional mostly did not hire a photographer. One selfie
> goes into WinkyPie, a set of photos for a dating profile comes back — different places,
> different light, same face and build. Pick a pose, it coaches the shot, then it renders you
> in it. Looks pro. Still you.

Headline: **Authentic pro photos. More matches.** ·
Description: **One selfie. No photographer.**

**Headline changed 2026-09-22 to the locked primary brand line** (PRODUCT.md §1, decided
2026-09-06, *Authentic* added 2026-09-07). It was "Looks pro. Still you." — also a locked
line, but the product-truth one, which answers a question this ad is not asking. This creative
promises matches; the headline should say so in the form the product has already sanctioned.

**Verbatim, 35 characters.** Not "Better photos. More matches.", not "Good photos, more
matches." §1 says the locked lines are identical everywhere and are not drifted, and
[[WP_P2_STATIC_swipingback]] was demoted to `v0-drifted` on the same day for paraphrasing this
exact line twice. Under Meta's ~40-character link-card cut with room to spare.

**It also does one thing for the waivers.** The frame closes on "x10 the matches" — an
unsourced multiplier, and one of the four items being carried. The headline makes the same
promise in the form the product is allowed to make it: a claim with no number attached to it.
That does not undo the waiver, but it means the ad's own link card shows what the compliant
version of that sentence looks like.

The opening sentence of the primary text is the peer-discovery beat rewritten as a statement
about a group rather than a story about a person — which is the whole difference between this
and the render we have.

**The text carries no story, and that is deliberate.** The frame already tells one; repeating
it in the primary text would double the exposure of the part that is being waived. Here the
text does the job the frame cannot — the mechanism, in the third person, with nothing asserted
about the viewer.

## Ratios — decided 2026-09-22

**4:5 only, placements open, crop accepted.** Meta does not reject a mismatched ratio: in
Stories and Reels it applies *Smart Zoom*, cropping roughly 15 % off each side, or it
letterboxes — and the advertiser does not choose which.

This piece takes it. Both text plates and the closing lock-up are centred on the middle axis
and survive either. What a Smart Zoom trims is the outer edge of the notification cards — the
app label on the left, the timestamp on the right — which costs detail, not argument.

A purpose-built 9:16 would still be the cheapest in the set to make, because the content is a
vertical scroll already: it needs restacking, not re-composing. Worth doing if this creative
earns a second round, and it goes in through per-placement asset customisation rather than as
a second ad.
