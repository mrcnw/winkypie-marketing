# Brief — WP_P3_STATIC_versus

**Single variable tested:** whether the switcher's angle — *most AI photo apps charge you for
the bad result* — buys the same result as a **design-only static** that it does as a paid
UGC shoot. Campaign 6 ([[WP_P3_UGC_triedthemall]]) puts that angle in a man's mouth for the
cost of a creator; this card says it in six lines for the cost of an afternoon. Same persona,
same angle, same closer. **The variable is the format, not the hook** — the one place in the
set where that is deliberate, and the cheapest question we can ask.

**Evidence:** the model card, [[Roast Comparison Card 2026-09-09]] (`2213072699509422`), went
live 2026-05-06 and is **still delivering on 2026-09-09 — 126 days** on a page that has burned
~960 creatives. Its twin, the before/after card, was created 27 seconds later and is also
still running: the two oldest living statics on the leader's page are both six-bullet cards.
The fill is ours — every one of their six lines fails a guardrail ([[Comparison Card Bullets]]
carries the axis decision, the pool of twelve candidate rows and the two blockers).

## Hook (verbatim, must be legible in 1.5 s, sound off)

> Most AI photo apps charge you for **the bad result**.

Passed the rubric in [[Hooks And Angles]] (Backup) on 2026-09-02 and had sat there unused. It
names the ✗ column, so hook and grid are one argument rather than two: **the grid is the
definition of "the bad result"** — it looks like AI, it is someone else's build, it came out
of their pack instead of your idea. Read that way the hook is the claim and the three ✗ lines
are the itemisation.

*the bad result* is the italic-gradient punch (the one brand gradient) — **italic gradient,
never uppercase**; caps are not the signature move (`BRAND.md` §4).

Sub, smaller, `#8A8A8A`: **"Same face. New level."** — the honest replacement for Roast's
"Same face. 10x the matches." ([[Hooks And Angles]]).

**The one open loop, and how it is closed.** "charge you" sets a money expectation that no row
on the card answers — the ✓ column is about output, not price. It is closed off-card, in the
primary text and by the App Store badge, and it is the reason the free-check row is the named
first swap-in below. Alternative hook if the loop should close on-frame instead: "Most AI photo
apps hand you **the bad result**." Third-person-safe variant: "Most AI photo apps charge for
the bad result."

Alt hook, already approved in this set: "**Someone else's face.** That's most AI photo apps." —
the host read for campaign 6 in [[Creative Naming]] (`WP_P3_HOST_triedthemall`). Swapping to it
makes the static and the UGC clip open on the same sentence, which sharpens the format test.

No competitor is named, ever. "Most AI photo apps" is a category described in four words —
never a brand, never a logo, never a greyed-out rival icon.

## The grid — the six lines, verbatim

Column heads in Inter, uppercase, `#8A8A8A` left and white right:

| ✗ **MOST AI PHOTO APPS** | ✓ **WINKYPIE** |
|---|---|
| AI that looks like AI. | Realistic photos. |
| Someone else's build. | Your body. |
| Fixed style packs. | Custom poses. |

**Read across, not down.** Each row is one dimension answered twice — how the photo looks,
whose body is in it, who chooses the shot. That single rule is what makes the model card scan
in under two seconds. Test before export: cover the right column, and the left has to stand on
its own as three things a product category does badly.

**Row order.** Likeness first — "it didn't look like me" is the #1 bad review at every paid
rival ([[Competitor Landscape]]), so the objection already in his head gets answered before we
ask for anything. Body second, because it is the sharpest and the most literally true line on
the card. Control last, above the CTA.

Register: both columns are short noun phrases with hard stops, sentence case, never Title
Case — Title-Case feature labels are App Store register, not the voice in PRODUCT.md §9.

What each line rests on:

| Row | ✓ rests on | ✗ rests on |
|---|---|---|
| Look | §1 — "the AI renders *you* in that pose and setting"; §5.2 selfie-mirroring, so the composition is real rather than a head on a stock body | The customer's own words in rivals' one-star reviews: "cartoonish", "plastic" ([[ROAST]], Trustpilot) |
| Body | §1 "real face, real build", and the §11.2 disclosure's own wording — "your actual face and body" | ROAST's Trustpilot theme #1 verbatim: "body they used was a small skinny guy, looked nothing like me". Roast's August ads say the mechanism out loud — "using other guys' photos" ([[Roast AI Creative Teardown 2026-09-06]]) |
| Control | §5 differentiator 1 + `#uploadyourpose` — upload any reference you have seen, saved under "Your Poses" | PRODUCT.md §5: "Most rivals ship fixed style packs only" |

**Two limits on this fill, both to be held in design and in every later variant:**

1. **"Realistic photos." never becomes "indistinguishable from a real photo", "nobody can
   tell" or anything adjacent.** That collides with §11.5 (honest about AI, "stylized AI
   representations, not a filter") and with platform AI labelling. The line claims the output
   looks like a photograph of him; it never claims the viewer cannot tell it is AI.
2. **"Custom poses." means *upload your own reference*, not a catalogue size.** No number, no
   "hundreds", no "every pose" — the catalog is backend-managed (§11.8, guardrail 7).

Safe-form swaps if review pushes back on the second person: "Your build." for "Your body.";
"Photos that read as photos." for "Realistic photos."

## What this fill costs, and the one swap to make first

Recorded so the trade is a decision and not an accident. The three ✓ lines chosen on
2026-09-09 describe **the output**. The three they replaced described **the mechanism**:

| Replaced row | Why it was there | Why losing it costs |
|---|---|---|
| "Coached before the shot." | Pose coaching, §5.1 | **Unique on the scoreboard** — nobody ships it, ReGen advertises the opposite |
| "Free check before it renders." | Free pre-flight check, §5.4 | **Unique**, answers the category's #2 complaint, and in the model card the free row is the one that touches the CTA |
| "Looks pro. Still you." | Locked product-truth line, §1 | Locked line, zero interpretation risk |

Of the three new lines, **"Your body." is unanswerable** — it is our mechanism stated as an
outcome. "Custom poses." is true but **not unique**: Photo AI ships "Copycat" and TruShot
"recreate a photo from Instagram/Pinterest" ([[Competitor Landscape]]). "Realistic photos." is
the claim every rival in the category already makes and fails; it cannot be verified from the
card and a competitor can paste it into their own grid tomorrow.

**First swap-in, if this card underperforms:** row 3 becomes
`Pay first. See later.` / `Free check before it renders.` — it restores the free row directly
above the App Store badge, which is what the 126-day model does with "Free", and puts a
mechanism nobody can copy back on the card. Rows 1 and 2 stay.

## Layout (single frame)

- Dark `#0E0E0E`, one warm amber-pink radial glow behind the type block (PRODUCT.md §10).
- Hook top, Fraunces, two lines, centred, the punch phrase italic in `135deg, #F59E0B → #EC4899`.
- Sub beneath it, Inter, `#8A8A8A`.
- **The grid: two columns, a 1 px `#262626` rule between them, three rows.** Marks in
  `#FF453A` left and `#34C759` right — the app's own error and success tokens (§10), not emoji.
  Bullets in Inter, one line each, never wrapping. If a line wraps at 4:5, cut a word.
- **No logo on the ✗ side.** The column head is text.
- The WinkyPie mark and wordmark sit on the right column head — the only logo on the card.
- Footer: "Be the right swipe." — the locked closer — then the App Store badge.
- **No person on frame and no rendered result** → no §11.2 disclosure needed. The moment
  anyone adds a result image, the disclosure comes with it and this brief is amended.
- Optional motion version: the three rows fade in top to bottom, 6 s loop, nothing else moves.

## Primary text (Meta placement)

> Most AI photo apps want ten selfies and hand back a face that isn't yours. WinkyPie renders
> your own selfie in the pose you picked — your face, your build — and checks the selfie for
> free before it renders anything. Authentic pro photos for your dating profile.

The free check lives here because it is off the card: it is the strongest risk-reversal we
have and it is what earns the tap.

Headline: **Looks pro. Still you.** · Description: **One selfie. No photographer.**

## Two blockers this brief exists to close

1. **No third-party marks.** The 2026-09-09 draft ran the Tinder and Hinge logos as the ✗
   column head. Those are Match Group marks in paid creative, and Meta lets rights holders pull
   ads through Brand Rights Protection; the exposure is an IP complaint against the ad account,
   not one rejected creative. Second reason to drop them: under those logos the card argues
   that Tinder and Hinge are the bad option, and our buyer is *on* Tinder and Hinge — we sell
   him a photo for that profile.
2. **Nothing on the card is about him.** Both column heads name a product category; the three
   ✗ lines describe what those products produce, not what he does, owns or fails at. That is
   the §11 form, and it is the difference between this card and the model it copies.

## Not on this card

| Tempting row | Why it stays off |
|---|---|
| "10 selfies, then wait." / "One selfie." | True (ROAST asks 4–10, Retake trains on 12) but one-selfie is **not unique** — GIO and Pose AI both take one. It lives in the primary text instead |
| "Someone else's photo. Your face." | The sharpest line we own — Roast says it out loud in four August listicles — but on cold traffic it teaches the viewer that face-swap is an option. Retargeting only |
| Anything about speed, price or privacy | Speed is unmeasured (§13.5); price is parked and no ad in the niche runs on it; the privacy contrast needs an unsourced claim about rivals |
| Any number, rating, user count or pose count | The app has no ratings yet (§11.1, until 50+ reviews); the catalog is backend-managed (§11.8) |

## Guardrail check (§11)

Third person about the product ✓ · nothing asserted about the viewer's appearance, results,
status or finances — "Your body." says whose body the output uses, in the disclosure's own
wording, and asserts nothing about it ✓ · AI described honestly; "realistic" never drifts to
"undetectable" ✓ · no competitor named, no third-party mark, no rival's UI ✓ · no stat, no
percentage, no user count, no rating ✓ · no dollar figure and no trial line ✓ · no pose count
✓ · no person on frame → no §11.2 needed ✓ · one gradient ✓ · men only — nothing on frame
depicts anyone ✓.
