# Brief — WP_P2_STATIC_checkcard

**Single variable tested:** whether the free-check angle — *it tells you the selfie won't work
before it charges you* — buys the same result as a **design-only static** that it does as a
screen-recorded demo. Campaign 2 ([[WP_P2_DEMO_freecheck]]) sells that mechanism in 20–25
seconds of captured flow; this card sells it in two frames and six lines. Same persona, same
hook, same closer. **The variable is the format, not the hook** — the same deliberate
exception the grid card makes ([[WP_P3_STATIC_versus]]), asked of the other unique mechanism.

**Evidence:** the model card, [[Roast Before After Card 2026-09-09]] (`1321304576602871`),
went live 2026-05-06 and is **still delivering on 2026-09-09 — 126 days**; it was created 27
seconds after the grid card and both are still running, which makes the two oldest living
statics on a ~960-ad page both six-bullet cards. The fill is ours: their headline asserts the
viewer's dating results, their footer invents proof, their mockup runs a rival's UI and their
first bullet claims a detection we do not have. The candidate rows, the layout options and the
number audit are in [[Before After Card Bullets]].

**Not campaign 4.** [[WP_P3_POSERESULT_stillyou]] already owns the *pose → result* static —
"left = pose reference, right = result". This card is *check → result*: the left frame is the
app refusing to render, with its reason on screen. Different mechanism, different persona,
no overlap. If the check screen cannot be shot honestly (see Production flags), this brief is
parked rather than re-pointed at campaign 4's layout.

## Hook (verbatim, must be legible in 1.5 s, sound off)

> Why you're not getting **matches**.

**Locked 2026-09-09 by the owner.** It is the model card's own headline and the one line on any
of our creatives that asserts the viewer's dating results — Meta's personal-attributes rule and
PRODUCT.md §11 both forbid it, and it is the most common rejection cause in this category. It
ships anyway, as a decision, with the compliant twin standing by. *matches* is the
italic-gradient punch (the one brand gradient) — **italic gradient, never uppercase**; caps are
not the signature move (`BRAND.md` §4).

**Fallback, ready to swap on the first rejection:** "Why photos don't get **matches**." — same
shape, same energy, subject moved off the man and onto the photo. Rendered as variant C.

Sub, smaller, `#8A8A8A`: **"One selfie. It checks it first — free."**

Alt hook, campaign 2's own and rubric-passed in [[Hooks And Angles]]: "Would your selfie
**pass**?" — use it only with the WON'T RENDER / RENDERED label pair below, where the check is
on frame; with neutral labels it promises a mechanism the card does not show.

The question is aimed at the *selfie*, never at the man, and never at his profile: we check
the selfie we are about to render, we do not audit a dating account. That is Roast's and
Charmd's mechanic, not ours — nothing on this card may imply otherwise
([[WP_P2_DEMO_freecheck]]).

Alt hook if the diagnostic question needs a rest: "Why a first photo works." ·
"What a first photo has to pass."

## The two frames

| | Left | Right |
|---|---|---|
| Label above | **THE SELFIE** | **THE PHOTO** |
| Frame | The Photo Check screen: his selfie beside the target pose he picked, verdict and reasons visible on screen | The generated result, full-frame |
| Device | A plain iPhone mockup running **the WinkyPie UI** | Same mockup, same UI |

**Never the words BEFORE and AFTER.** They are what turns this layout into an appearance
claim; the labels name what is in each frame, not what he looked like. "The selfie" and "the
photo" also state the product in two words: one goes in, the other comes out.

**The label pair decides what the bullets may say.** With `THE SELFIE / THE PHOTO` the bullets
caption the difference between the two frames and anything visible is fair. Swap in
`WON'T RENDER / RENDERED` — the variant that puts the free check on frame, paired with the
"Would your selfie pass?" hook — and every ✗ has to be a real check reason: row 3 then becomes
`Eyes hidden.`, because "won't render" beside "No smile." implies the app blocks a man for not
smiling, which it does not.

**The same man in both frames.** That is the product's claim, not a risk to manage — and it is
the line PRODUCT.md §11.5 draws: never imply the two images show different people.

**The left frame is technically bad, never facially bad.** Backlit, in shadow, eyes hidden,
badly framed for the pose he picked. It is a selfie the app refused, with the reason printed
on it — not an unflattering photograph of a man.

**No dating-app interface anywhere.** The model card runs a fake Tinder UI inside both
mockups; ours runs the product we own, which sells the mechanism at the same time.

**Read across, not down** — one dimension per row: light, eyes, pose. And the rule this format
adds on top: **every line has to be verifiable in the two frames.** A bullet the eye cannot
check is dead weight here, because in this layout the photographs carry the argument and the
words only name what is already visible. Test before export: cover the text and the two frames
still have to tell the story.

Register: both columns short, hard stops, sentence case — never Title Case.

What each line rests on:

| Row | ✓ rests on | ✗ rests on |
|---|---|---|
| Pose | Selfie-mirroring, §5.2 — **the moat in one line** | The check's actual job: *this* selfie against *that* pose, both visible in the left frame |
| Light | §6 collections deliver real locations and real light — `#citystreet`, `#cafedrinks`, `#goldenhour`. Taught, with sources, on slide 7 of [[WP_IG_EDU1_firstphoto]] | The check returns pass / warn / block with a reason (§4, step 3); light is one of them |
| Expression | Expression is one of the four coaching fields — body angle, gaze, hands, expression (§4, step 2) | Visible in the left frame. **Not a check reason** — which is why the neutral label pair is the primary one |

Swap-ins, all from the pool in [[Before After Card Bullets]], all still visible in frame:
`Eyes hidden.` / `Eyes to the lens.` (the row the WON'T RENDER variant needs) · `Thirty metres

the placeholder left frame. Same card, three headlines, and the bullet set changes with them.

| Variant | Headline | Bullets | Verdict |

| **B** `…-B` | "Why you're not getting **matches**." | No posing skills. · Bad lighting. · Bad frame. → Your pose, mirrored. · Real place, real light. · One face, chest up. | **The owner's call, and the one line on any of our cards that asserts the viewer's dating results.** It is the model card's own headline; Meta's personal-attributes rule and §11 both forbid it, and it is the most common rejection cause in this category. Rendered so the choice is made on the real thing, not on a description of it |
| **C** `…-C` | "Why photos don't get **matches**." | Same as B | The compliant twin of B — same energy, subject moved off the man and onto the photo. The control to run B against, and the fallback when B is rejected |

**Why the likeness lines live here and not on campaign 8 (revised 2026-09-09).** The ✓ column
was first written to answer the ✗ column row by row — light, eyes, frame. The owner's set puts
the likeness claims here instead: *Your build.* and *Looks pro. Still you.* The reason it is
right is evidence, not symmetry. **Campaign 8 is text-only, so a likeness claim there is
asserted; this card carries two frames of the same man, so the same claim is demonstrated.**
Proof belongs where the photographs are. The columns then read as blocks rather than pairs —
left, what is wrong with the selfie; right, what comes out of the app — with the likeness beat
last, directly above the closer.

Wording fixed at the same time: "Mirror" → **"Mirrors"** (without the *s* it is an imperative
aimed at the viewer, which §11 does not allow), "Your body." → **"Your build."** (§1's own word,
unambiguous, male-coded), and "Realistic photos." → **"Looks pro. Still you."** (the locked
line from §1; "realistic" is the promise the whole category makes and the whole category fails
on, and the frames prove the claim better than the word does).

**The one asset condition:** *Your build.* is true of the specific pair on the card or it is not
true at all. Compare shoulders, weight and build across the two frames before export; if the
render slimmed or broadened him, the line comes off that asset. Body fidelity is not measured
anywhere (§13), so this check is manual and per-creative.

**What the ✗ column still decides.** On this card the ✗ column is *his own selfie*, so
every ✓ has to answer that same fault. Output lines like "Your body." or "Realistic photos."
answer a different opponent — *other AI photo apps* — which is campaign 8's argument
([[WP_P3_STATIC_versus]]). Moving them here makes both cards say one thing in two layouts and
costs us one of the two tests. The ✗ column decides who the opponent is; the ✓ column has to
answer that opponent, row by row.

The B/C bullet set is itself an improvement on A: three rows, three dimensions, all visible in
frame — skill, light, frame — and the third carries the §8 "+38 % · Image-engagement 2025" label
if a number is ever wanted. "No posing skills." is a noun phrase about a situation, not a
protected attribute; the safest form remains "Guessing the pose."

## Layout (single frame)

- Dark `#0E0E0E`, one warm amber-pink radial glow behind the type block (PRODUCT.md §10).
- Hook top, Fraunces, centred, the punch word italic in `135deg, #F59E0B → #EC4899`. Sub in
  Inter, `#8A8A8A`.
- Two device mockups side by side, equal size, equal crop, **no lighter panel behind the
  winning side** — the model card tilts the frame in favour of "after" and that is part of how
  it overclaims.
- Labels above the frames in Inter, uppercase, `#8A8A8A` left and white right.
- Marks in `#FF453A` and `#34C759` — the app's own error and success tokens (§10), not emoji.
- Footer: "Be the right swipe." — the locked closer — then the App Store badge.
- **§11.2 disclosure strip along the bottom, Inter, `#8A8A8A`, mandatory**, because a rendered
  result is on the card: *"Demo. Your photos use your actual face and body. Results vary based
  on selfie quality, lighting, and pose."*
- Optional motion version: the left frame holds, the right resolves from the pass verdict to
  the result once, 6 s loop. Nothing else moves.

## Production flags (resolve before design starts)

1. **The check must produce an honest failing state** on a selfie that is technically bad —
   backlit, shadowed, eyes hidden, wrong angle for the pose — and not unflattering. If the app
   cannot be made to return that verdict on such a selfie, there is no left frame and this
   brief parks. This is the open question carried over from [[Before After Card Bullets]].
2. **One man, three assets:** the failing selfie, the passing selfie, the result — all the same
   person, all from one real session. Same dependency as campaign 7, and the same **likeness
   release** gap: no man's face runs in a paid ad without one ([[Host Scripts]], and the
   blocking item in [[03 TODO]]).
3. **Capture, not mockup.** Both screens are real app captures at 1206 × 2622 (`BRAND.md` §10),
   dropped into the device frame. Nothing about the verdict is redrawn in the design file.
4. The result shown is an AI demo asset, never a customer's photo and never framed as one.

## Do this, in order

1. Read the model card, [[Roast Before After Card 2026-09-09]]: take the two-frame geometry,
   the labels-above-the-frames structure and the caption-the-visible rule. Take none of its six
   lines, its headline or its footer.
2. Clear production flag 1 first — set up a demo profile, pick a pose, and shoot a selfie the
   check actually refuses, with the reason on screen. If that state does not exist honestly,
   stop and say so.
3. Capture the three screens on an iPhone: the block verdict with the target pose beside it,
   the pass verdict, the result. One session, one man, release signed.
4. Design the single frame in the brand kit (PRODUCT.md §10): hook with its gradient punch
   word, sub, two equal mockups with WON'T RENDER / RENDERED above them, three ✗ and three ✓,
   footer, badge, and the §11.2 strip.
5. Check the grid **reads across**, and cover the text to check the two frames still tell the
   story on their own.
6. Optional: the 6-second motion version — only the right frame resolves.
7. Export 9:16 (1080 × 1920), 4:5 (1080 × 1350) and 1:1 (1080 × 1080) with Meta's caption-crop
   safe margins; name `WP_P2_STATIC_checkcard_<ratio>_v1` per [[Creative Naming]].
8. Run the Guardrail check line by line; fix what fails, do not argue with it.
9. Register in the app's asset browser and hand to [[08 Launch The Ad]] with the hypothesis
   line written before spend.
