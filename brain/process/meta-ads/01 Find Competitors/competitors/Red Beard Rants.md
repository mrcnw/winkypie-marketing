# Red Beard Rants (redbeardrants.net/ai-photos)

**A dating-coach influencer selling done-for-you AI dating photos at $650–1,000 a pack, on
the promise that they are "100% undetectable".** Not an app, not a Meta advertiser, and not
our buyer's wallet — but the clearest live example of the *opposite* corner of the market:
per-person model training, week-long turnaround, and deception as the selling point. Read
it as a price anchor and an objection list, not as a threat. Analysed 2026-09-02.

## Meta Ad Library (checked live 2026-09-02, US)

**No ads found.** Searches for `redbeardrants` and `undetectable AI photos dating` return 0;
`Red Beard Rants` and `Casey Red Beard` match only unrelated pages. The offer is sold to his
own audience (IG → site → Whop), not bought with paid traffic. His "Instagram ads" line on the
homepage is a service he sells to clients (boosting *their* content to women), not ads for
this product. Re-check monthly: an "undetectable AI" creative on Meta would be a useful test
of how strictly the category is policed.

## The sample photos (looked at, not measured)

Nine images, one identity (shaved head, red beard), luxury-travel settings — resort pool,
rooftop jacuzzi at night, city terrace, bar, blazer-and-tee. Quality is **high**: coherent
lighting, believable phone-camera look, consistent face and build across scenes. The tells
are subtle (skin uniformity, hands in pockets in most frames, very even exposure). Honest
read: the output bar he shows is at or above what most apps show on their store pages. The
pixels are not where we beat him.

## Claims that do not hold

1. **"Meta and the dating apps 100% CANNOT DETECT."** Half a premise, no conclusion: Google
   does watermark Gemini images (SynthID) and OpenAI attaches C2PA metadata, so "public
   models are marked" is roughly true. That Tinder, Hinge or Bumble *scan for* those marks
   is asserted, never shown, and there is no public evidence of it. Stripping metadata is
   also what any screenshot or re-encode does — it is not proprietary.
2. **"1.5–2x / 10x better results"** — no source, no definition of "results".
3. **Refund** — checkout promises a partial refund the policy page forbids.
4. **"22 clients"** — unverifiable; the Whop store shows no reviews.

## The buyer's risk (why this offer is fragile)

- The three big apps ban misrepresentation, not AI as such; Bumble added an optional
  "AI-assisted" photo badge in March 2026 (reported by competitor blogs
  [pose.ai](https://pose.ai/blog/are-ai-photos-allowed-dating-apps-2026),
  [datephotos.ai](https://datephotos.ai/blog/ai-dating-photos-2026-new-rules) — secondary
  sources, treat as unconfirmed). An offer whose whole pitch is evading detection sells the
  customer a policy violation and the first-date reveal along with it.
- $150 of every order is non-refundable by his own words, and the policy page says all of it is.
- He needs a set of your real photos and trains a model on them; nothing on the site says
  what happens to either afterwards.

## Where WinkyPie wins / loses

- **Win — price and speed, by two orders of magnitude.** $50–65 a photo and 5–7 days versus
  a subscription with a 3-day trial and minutes per photo (PRODUCT.md §7; generation time
  still unmeasured, §13). Don't quote dollars in creative (§11.6), but this is the strongest
  anchor the savings pillar has found: someone charges $650 for ten AI photos and reports buyers.
- **Win — honesty is the compliant corner.** "Stylized AI representations, still you" versus
  "undetectable". As platforms move toward AI labels, his position ages badly and ours does not.
- **Win — input burden.** One coached selfie per pose versus a training set plus a scheduling
  call; free check before anything costs.
- **Lose — likeness ceiling.** A per-person fine-tune, done well, holds identity and body
  across many scenes; his samples show it. Our pose-mimic flow argues likeness through the
  real selfie, but we have no side-by-side evidence yet. Do not claim "better likeness".
- **Lose — distribution he owns.** ~93k IG + 18k YT of exactly-male, exactly-dating attention
  he can sell to for free. We have none yet.
- **Neither** has social proof: his "22 said no" is a privacy reframe of zero testimonials;
  our listing has no ratings (§13.4). His move is worth learning: he turned the proof gap
  into a trust statement.

## What it tells us beyond the offer

An influencer with a male dating audience has started monetising it with AI photos as a
done-for-you service. That is the shape channel 5 (influencers, queued) will meet: creators
who would rather sell their own $650 pack than promote a $19.99 app, unless the app pays
better per follower. Note it; do not open the channel for it. **This particular creator is
brand-unsafe for us** — the "hotter girls / passport bro" framing collides with PRODUCT.md §9
(never cringe, never body-shaming) and with Meta's rules.
