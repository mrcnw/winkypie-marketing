# Comparison Card Bullets

What may go in the three ✗ and three ✓ of a WinkyPie comparison card, scored the way
[[Hooks And Angles]] scores hooks. The model ad is taken apart in
[[Roast Comparison Card 2026-09-09]]; this note is only about what we are allowed and able to
put in the six lines. Its sister — the six bullets of a **before/after** card, where the
photographs carry the argument instead of the words — is [[Before After Card Bullets]]. Every ✓ traces to a shipped product fact in PRODUCT.md §5; every ✗ has
to survive PRODUCT.md §11 without asserting anything about the man looking at it.

## The pick

**✗ column head: "Most AI photo apps" · ✓ column head: "WinkyPie" (mark + wordmark).**
One dimension per row, read across:

| # | Dimension | ✗ Most AI photo apps | ✓ WinkyPie |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Likeness | Doesn't look like you. | **Looks pro. Still you.** |
| 2 | Skill | Guess the pose yourself. | **Coached before the shot.** |
| 3 | Money at risk | Pay first. See later. | **Free check before it renders.** |

Row 3 sits directly above the App Store badge — the free row touches the CTA, which is what
the 126-day model ad does with "Free".

Why these three and not any other three: they are **exactly the three differentiators the
[[Competitor Landscape]] scoreboard still marks unique** (pose coaching, selfie-mirroring
likeness, free pre-generation check) laid against **the three complaints every paid rival
collects** (doesn't look like me · pay before you see anything · billing traps). The ✗ column
is not invented — it is the rivals' own one-star reviews, and the ✓ column is not a promise —
it is three screens that exist in the app.

Safe-form variants, if a reviewer flags the second person: "A face that isn't quite yours." ·
"Pose it yourself." · "Paid before you see anything."

## Variant B — the status-quo card (cold traffic)

Same grid, ✗ column head **"The usual way"**, bullets written as noun phrases so they describe
a method and not his photographs:

| # | Dimension | ✗ The usual way | ✓ WinkyPie |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Source | A friend, a phone, ten tries. | One selfie, one pose, coached. |
| 2 | Skill | Guessing how to stand. | Body angle, gaze, hands — on screen. |
| 3 | Certainty | Hoping it works. | It checks the selfie first. Free. |

Ship this one second. It is the wider audience but the weaker argument: it never explains why
not the other AI app, and it is the version that has to be watched for personal attributes.

## Two blockers in the 2026-09-09 draft

**1 · The Tinder and Hinge logos have to come off.** They are third-party marks in paid
creative. US law does permit truthful comparative advertising that names a competitor, but a
competitor's *logo* carries no comparative purpose, and Meta's ad standards forbid content
that infringes third-party IP and let rights holders pull ads through Brand Rights Protection
— which Match Group runs. The exposure is not one rejected ad; it is an IP complaint against
the ad account we are about to spend on. Replace the two icons with a two-word text label.
There is a second reason: under those logos the card reads "Tinder and Hinge are the bad
option", and our buyer *is on* Tinder and Hinge. We sell him a photo for that profile.

**2 · The rows do not pair.** The draft reads down each column instead of across the grid:
"Group shots and mirror selfies" sits opposite "Coached before the shot" (input vs method),
"Guessing how to pose" opposite "Free check before it renders" (skill vs money), "Paying for
the bad result" opposite "Looks pro. Still you." (money vs likeness). The model card works
because both halves of a row answer the same question. Re-pair, and the same three ideas —
coaching, the free check, likeness — read in half the time. Separately: "Group shots and
mirror selfies" and "Paying for the bad result" both describe the viewer's own photos and
spending; moved to "Most AI photo apps" they describe a product category instead, which is
the form §11 asks for.

## Hook, closer, CTA

| Slot | Line | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | **"Most AI photo apps charge you for the bad result."** | Already rubric-passed in [[Hooks And Angles]] (Backup) and it names the ✗ column, so hook and grid are one argument. Punch words *the bad result* in the one brand gradient. Third-person-safe variant: "Most AI photo apps charge for the bad result." |
| Hook, alt | "Two ways to get a first photo." | Shorter, sets the grid up explicitly, says nothing about anyone |
| Sub | "Same face. New level." | Keep — the honest replacement for Roast's "Same face. 10x the matches." |
| Closer | "Be the right swipe." | Locked CTA line, above the badge |
| CTA | App Store badge | Ours is an install, not a quiz. No pill copy promising a free anything beyond the badge |

**Do not reuse campaign 5's hook here.** [[WP_P1_STATIC_algorithm]] exists to test
"Not the algorithm. The first photo." against campaign 1 in *the same layout* — its brief says
the two files must differ only in the first line. A comparison grid under that hook changes
hook and layout at once and destroys the read. The card is a **new creative for wave two**,
named per [[Creative Naming]] (`WP_P?_STATIC_versus_<ratio>_v1`); the persona and campaign
number get decided when it is briefed in step 03.

## Guardrail check (PRODUCT.md §11)

Third person about the product ✓ · nothing asserted about the viewer's appearance, results,
status or finances ✓ · no stat, no percentage ✓ · no dollar figure ✓ · no third-party mark
once the two icons come off ✓ · no invented proof, no rating, no user count ✓ · no person on
frame → no §11.2 disclosure needed; **the moment a result image is added, the disclosure comes
with it** ✓ · one gradient ✓ · no pose count ✓ · the check is a *selfie* check, never a
profile review ✓.
