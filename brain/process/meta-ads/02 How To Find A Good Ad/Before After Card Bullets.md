# Before After Card Bullets

What may go in the three ✗ and three ✓ of a WinkyPie before/after card. The model is taken
apart in [[Roast Before After Card 2026-09-09]]; the sister note for the six-bullet *grid*
card — a different format with a different bullet axis — is [[Comparison Card Bullets]].

The difference between the two notes in one line: **in the grid card the bullets carry the
argument; here the two photographs carry it and the bullets only name what the eye already
sees.** Every candidate below is judged on whether it is visible in the frame.

## The pick

**✗ head: "What the free check flags" · ✓ head: "What the coaching sets up".**
Both column heads name a mechanism of ours, so both columns are about the app and neither is
about the man looking at it.

| # | Dimension | ✗ What the free check flags | ✓ What the coaching sets up |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Light | Backlit. Face in shadow. | **Real place, real light.** |
| 2 | Eyes | Eyes hidden. | **Eyes to the lens.** |
| 3 | Pose | Doesn't match the pose. | **Mirrors the pose you picked.** |

Row 3 is the one no rival can write. Rows 1 and 2 are the two most visible attributes in any
first photo and both are already taught, with sources, on slides 4 and 7 of
[[WP_IG_EDU1_firstphoto]] — the paid card and the organic carousel then say the same thing in
the same words, which is the point.

## The layout — the real decision on this card

The bullets are easy; what the two images are is not. Roast's left phone is a dark cracked-screen
mirror selfie and the right is a golden-hour portrait of a **visibly different, better-groomed
man**. The bullets say "lighting"; the picture says "another person". PRODUCT.md §11.5 forbids
exactly that implication and §11 tells us to prefer *reference pose → result*.

| | Left frame | Right frame | For | Against | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | The **Photo Check screen**: his selfie beside the target pose, verdict showing, with the reasons on screen | The pass verdict and the rendered result | The ✗ side is not "an unattractive man", it is **a selfie the app refused to render, with its reason printed on it** · shows the one mechanism nobody else has · **same face on both sides, which is our claim, not our risk** · reuses the capture [[WP_P2_DEMO_freecheck]] already calls for | Still a before/after of a man's face in an appearance-adjacent category · §11.2 disclosure mandatory · the ✗ frame must be *technically* bad (dim, backlit, cropped) and never *facially* bad | **Pick** |
| **B** | The pose reference from the catalog plus the coaching card | The rendered result | The framing §11 names by name · no photograph of him on the losing side at all · zero likeness risk | Loses the dark→bright gut-punch that makes the format work sound-off · asks a cold viewer to understand what a "pose reference" is | Variant — test second, and the safe fallback if A is rejected |
| **C** | His current dating photo | The result | Strongest contrast | "Bad me → hot me". Banned by §11 | Never |

**Non-negotiable in A:** the same man in both frames, the check's own reason visible on the
left, and the §11.2 results-vary line on the card from the moment a rendered photo appears.
The mockup shows the **WinkyPie** UI — never a dating app's interface.

## Hook, footer, CTA

| Slot | Line | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | **"Why a first photo works."** | Keeps the model's diagnostic frame, moves the subject from him to the photo. Punch word *works* in the one brand gradient. The model's "Why you're not getting matches" asserts his results and is the single most common rejection cause in this category |
| Hook, alt | "What a first photo has to pass." | Sets the ✗/✓ grid up explicitly and echoes the free check |
| Hook, alt | "First photo. First chance." | The locked problem line (§1) — safest, least new |
| Labels | "WON'T RENDER" / "RENDERED" (layout A) · "THE POSE" / "YOUR PHOTO" (layout B) | Never "BEFORE / AFTER" — the two words are what makes the format an appearance claim |
| Footer | "Be the right swipe." | The locked closer, in the slot where the model puts its user count |
| CTA | App Store badge | Ours is an install. No quiz, no "see what's wrong for free" |
| Disclosure | "Demo. Your photos use your actual face and body. Results vary based on selfie quality, lighting, and pose." | §11.2 — mandatory from the moment a rendered photo is on the card |

## Guardrail check (PRODUCT.md §11)

Third person about the product and the photo, nothing asserted about the viewer ✓ · no user
count, no rating, no testimonial ✓ · numbers only from §8 and only with their label ✓ · no
dollar figure ✓ · no third-party mark and no imitation of a dating app's UI ✓ · **same man in
both frames — the card must never imply a different person** ✓ · §11.2 disclosure on frame ✓ ·
male subject ✓ · one gradient ✓ · no pose count ✓ · the check is a selfie check, never a
profile review ✓.
