The AIGC line sits on every single result frame, small, bottom-left, for the whole 27 seconds.

## Mechanism (the three lines)

- **First 1.5 s:** a full-frame magazine portrait with an ordinary man talking in the corner
  of it. No title card, no question, no product. The gap between the corner and the frame is
  the entire hook and it needs no sound.
- **Tension:** these look like a paid shoot with a stylist. He says he has never done one.
- **Asked to believe:** one selfie returns a whole series, and it is still him.

## What this cut teaches that the female one does not

1. **The results can be the whole ad.** Nineteen seconds of narration over thirteen finished
   photos, and the app is never shown — no screen recording, no UI, no upload flow, no
   before/after. Every creative in our round-one set explains a mechanism and reveals one
   result at the end. This one inverts it: proof first, mechanism never. That is a variable
   we have not tested, and it is the cheapest one to produce because the assets are the
   product's own output.
2. **"My buddy saw these and asked who shot them."** Third-party recognition as proof —
   no number, no rating, no testimonial claim about results. The one social-proof mechanism
   that survives §11.1, *if it actually happened*. Note the line the market gives us for
   free: the compliment is about the photograph, never about the man.
3. **A persistent AI disclosure does not kill the format.** The AIGC line rides every frame
   of a creative built entirely on generated imagery, and the account still posts it as its
   main cut. Our §11.2 line is shorter than theirs.
4. **Casting is their A/B, not their message.** Two cuts twelve seconds apart, identical
   otherwise. Ours is fixed male by guardrail 1 — which means we get their male learning for
   free and never spend on the other half.
5. **Wardrobe range is the variety proof.** Suit → tank top → leather → café → street. The
   wall reads as "a profile's worth" because no two frames repeat a look, not because anyone
   says so.

## What we never copy

- **The shirtless gym-body frames.** Several results are bare-torso abs shots. That is the
  body-insecurity register PRODUCT.md §9 rules out, and it stops the ad being about photos.
- **"Realistic, sharp, professional"** as adjectives claimed by the narrator — ours has to be
  shown by the frame or not said.
- **"I'm not a model. I've never done a photoshoot"** in a generated mouth. First-person
  experience stays human ([[Production Guardrails]] Lane B rule 1).
- **"Try it from one picture. Try it yourself."** — a CTA with nothing behind it. Ours names
  the free check.
