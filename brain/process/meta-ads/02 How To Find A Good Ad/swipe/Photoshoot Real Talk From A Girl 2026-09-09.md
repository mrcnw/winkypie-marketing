# Photoshoot Dating — "real talk from a girl", 76 seconds of one face (2026-08-17)

The 4th-ranked ad by total impressions on ROAST's second page, and the **longest creative
anyone in the swipe file runs: 1:16**. One unbroken selfie take of a woman talking to camera,
front-lit, indoors — and for 76 seconds the frame never leaves her face. Every piece of proof
is an *overlay dropped on top of her*, never a cut away from her. It is the opposite of
[[Reface Male Portfolio Cut 2026-09-08]], which spends 26 of its 32 seconds on results and
almost none on a person.

Two things make it worth the teardown even though it breaks half our guardrails. First, the
whole ad is one hook we already own — **the half-second decision** — argued for a full minute
instead of stated in three words. Second, the argument is delivered by the person who does the
deciding, which is a credibility source we have not costed.

Files: `~/Downloads/photoshoot-dating-ads-2026-09-09/` — `media/1042679478407810.mp4`,
`transcripts/`, `frames/`. Both ads torn down on 2026-09-09 share the folder.

**The before/after is the smartest thing in the ad and it is easy to miss.** The "before" is
not an ugly photo. It is a *stiff corporate headshot* — good lighting, bad register. The
"after" is the same man, same face, relaxed, on a terrace. They are not selling
ugly → handsome. They are selling **wrong register → right register**, which is the one
before/after framing that does not read as "a different person" and does not trip Meta's
appearance rules. Compare `PRODUCT.md` §11.5.

**No end card.** It ends mid-thought on her face at 76.1 s. Everything the viewer is meant to
do lives in the ad unit around the video, not inside it.

## Mechanism (the three lines)

- **First 1.5 s:** a woman's face fills the phone, mid-sentence, with a *censored* photo of a
  man pinned under her chin. The pixelation is the hook — it reads as someone's real profile
  being discussed without permission, and you stay to see whose.
- **Tension:** she is the judge, and she is about to tell you the criterion. Then she removes
  the two you feared (abs, money) and replaces them with one you can actually change.
- **Asked to believe:** the reason you get no matches is a production problem, not you — and a
  woman is the one saying it, so it is not the seller's opinion.

## Mix

Measured with `ffmpeg -af ebur128`, 2026-09-09.

- **−14.2 LUFS integrated, LRA 3.5 LU.** Voice only — no music bed, no stings, no ducking to
  measure, because there is nothing to duck under. The 3.5 LU range is a single close-mic
  voice, lightly compressed.
- **The ad is unwatchable silent.** Every argument is spoken; the karaoke captions carry it,
  but the captions run one word at a time and cannot be skimmed. This fails our Silent test
  outright and they ship it anyway at 1:16 — which tells us their placement is a feed where
  sound plays, not a Reels-style sound-off scroll.

## Our version

The format is available to us, the casting is not — and that is the whole cost of copying it.

**What ports:**

1. **The one-take talking argument at length.** Nothing about a 76-second single take is
   expensive: a phone, a window, and someone who can hold an argument. It is the cheapest
   creative in the swipe file to produce and the most expensive to *cast*.
2. **Register-swap before/after.** Stiff corporate headshot → relaxed real-setting photo, same
   face, same shirt-collar man. Safe under `PRODUCT.md` §11.5 in a way "bad me → hot me" never
   is, and it happens to be exactly what our pose catalog does. This is the single most
   reusable thing in the ad.
3. **Removing the feared causes before naming the real one.** "Not abs, not a six-figure job —
   your photos." Our pillar 1 ("Same face. New level.") states the conclusion; this states the
   *eliminations* first and earns it. Worth one test as a hook variant on
   [[WP_P2_STATIC_100ms]].
4. **The full-frame grid takeover, ~0.9 s per layout.** Three grids in under three seconds
   does the Variety pillar (§9, weight 12%) without a word of copy.

**What has to change before anything ships:**

- **Guardrail 1: men only.** Our narrator cannot be a woman on screen. That removes the
  ad's whole credibility engine — "here's how it works on our side" only works from the side
  that swipes. The honest port is a **male narrator arguing about photographs**, not about
  women, which is a weaker but compliant claim. Do not fake it with a female voiceover over
  male footage either; the guardrail is about representation, and half-measures read worse.
- **The hypergamy chart is a hard no.** It is a redpill artefact, it carries an implied
  statistic with no source (§8, "cite or drop it"), and it moves the ad from "your photos are
  fixable" to "the market is rigged against you". Wrong emotion (§9: avoid body insecurity and
  overwhelm), wrong audience, and unusable in a brand we want to keep.
- **"Girls swipe right on maybe five percent"** — an invented number in a mouth. §11.1 and
  §11.4. Never.
- **Second-person appearance and relationship claims.** "You swipe for hours, match with no
  one, start thinking you're not attractive enough" is a textbook Meta personal-attributes
  rejection (§11, Meta specifically). Ours has to be third person about the product.
- **The pixelated-profile hook.** Reads as a real man's profile being publicly judged. Even
  if the photo is stock, the *device* is public roasting, and it is not the brand.
- **No AI disclosure.** They ship 76 seconds of generated imagery with none. We label
  ("Stylized AI representations, not a filter", §11.5) and we take the small hook cost.

**Recommended:** a **60–75 s male-narrator cut** built on beats 1, 2 and 4 above, as a
sixth-campaign test, not a replacement for anything in the round-one set. It is the only
long-form slot we would have, and everything shorter is already covered. Brief it against
[[WP_P2_STATIC_100ms]]'s territory so the hook line stays the locked one.
