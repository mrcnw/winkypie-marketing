## Mechanism

- **First 1.5 s:** two phones, dark against bright. The contrast is read before a single word;
  the bullets are captions for a difference already seen.
- **Tension:** the difference between the two phones is three small, fixable, *technical*
  things — not his face. Anyone can see which phone wins.
- **Asked to believe:** those three attributes are why the swipes are not coming, and 724,000
  men have already made the swap.

**Every bullet is verifiable in the two images.** That is the rule this format lives by, and it
is what separates it from the grid card: there, the bullets carry the argument; here, the
photographs carry it and the bullets only name what you are looking at. A bullet that cannot
be seen in the frame is dead weight in this layout.

**The bullet axis is the photo, not the product.** Nothing in the six lines mentions Roast,
AI, speed or price. The product enters only through the CTA — "See what's wrong for free" —
and through the footer's number. It is an education card with a proof line stapled to it.

**Asymmetric weight.** The ✗ side is a phone-flash mirror selfie behind a cracked screen; the
✓ side is a golden-hour portrait of a visibly better-looking, better-groomed man. The bullets
say "lighting" and "smile"; the image says "different man". That gap is the deception this
format invites, and it is precisely where our guardrails bite.

## Copy this

1. **Bullets as captions for a visible difference.** Nothing on the card that the eye cannot check.
2. **Three technical, fixable attributes** — light, framing, expression. Not the face.
3. **The label pair above the phones** does the structural work; the hook does not have to.
4. **Two identical device frames**, one variable between them.
5. **A short footer line under the grid** as the last thing read before the CTA.

## Never copy this

| Theirs | Why |
|---|---|
| "Why you're not getting matches" | Asserts the viewer's dating results — the personal-attributes line in PRODUCT.md §11, and the single most common rejection cause in this category |
| "724,000+ guys already fixed this" | Self-reported, uncitable, and for us simply invented — banned by §11.1 until 50+ real reviews exist. Their own landing says 820,000 the same week ([[ROAST]]) |
| The Tinder wordmark and swipe UI inside the mockup | A third-party mark plus an imitation of another product's interface. Ours shows the WinkyPie result screen, which we own and which sells the product anyway |
| BEFORE / AFTER of a man's face | Restricted in appearance-adjacent categories; §11 tells us to run **"reference pose → result"** instead of "bad me → hot me" |
| A rendered result with no disclosure | §11.2 requires the results-vary line on any before/after imagery. They ship none |
| "Old photo" as a ✗ | Not something a WinkyPie check can flag. Our pre-flight compares the selfie to the chosen pose — claiming it detects photo age is a false product claim |
