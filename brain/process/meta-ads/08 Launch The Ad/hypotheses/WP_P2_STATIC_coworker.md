# Hypothesis — WP_P2_STATIC_coworker

**Committed 2026-09-22 by the owner, before any spend.** Not edited again: if the prediction
turns out wrong, that is the result ([[08 Launch The Ad]]).

## The line

> If we show the **peer-discovery story** to **P2 Grinder**, then it **beats
> [[WP_P2_STATIC_oneselfie]] on link CTR**, because a claim that somebody else already found
> this is a stronger reason to tap than a demonstration of how it works.

## Read delivery first, and do not mix the two

This creative carries **four waived §11 failures** (see the brief). So the first thing it
measures is not CTR — it is **whether it serves at all**. Keep the stages apart:

1. **Delivery.** Approved and running, or rejected. A rejection is a result about the four
   carried items, not about peer discovery, and it settles nothing about the angle.
2. **CTR against campaign 11**, among impressions that actually served.

Campaign 11 is the right comparison because it is the same mechanism without the story frame:
proof shown rather than claimed, no fabricated person, no number. The pair answers what the
story frame is worth — which is the only question this campaign was cut to ask.

## What would falsify it

Served, and at or below campaign 11 on CTR. That says the story frame buys nothing, and since
the story frame is the only part carrying the waivers, the answer is to run campaign 11 and
retire this one rather than re-cut it.

## The asymmetry worth naming before the numbers arrive

If it **wins**, the win is not transferable as it stands: the parts doing the work are the
parts that cannot stay. A win means cutting a version that keeps the peer-discovery beat
without the fabricated person, the multiplier or the third-party interface — the three rules
in the brief — and testing whether the angle survives losing them.
