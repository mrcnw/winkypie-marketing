# 08 · Launch The Ad

Checklist: [[08 TODO]] · Drawing: [[Process.excalidraw|Process]]

## Goal
Five campaigns live, each with its hypothesis written down *before* spend started, and a
stated date until which nobody touches anything.

## Process
1. **Hypothesis before spend, in writing.** Format: *"If we show <hook> to <persona>, then
   <metric> beats <baseline>, because <reason>."* A campaign without one produces an
   anecdote, not a result — and hypotheses written after the fact always turn out to have
   predicted whatever happened.
2. **Launch them together.** Staggered launches make campaigns incomparable: different
   auction conditions, different days, different competition.
3. **Then leave it alone.** No edits during the learning phase. Not the budget, not the
   creative, not the targeting. Editing resets learning and burns the spend already made.
   Write the date on the tracker so the rule is visible, not remembered.
4. **Day-one check is delivery, not performance.** Is it spending? Are impressions served?
   Are events arriving? Performance numbers on day one are noise and reading them is how
   good campaigns get killed.
5. **If something is genuinely broken — a rejection, a tracking failure, spend at zero —**
   fix it and note that this campaign's clock restarted. It is now not comparable to the
   other four; say so rather than quietly averaging it in.
6. **Log the timestamp.** Launch time is needed to know when the learning phase ends and
   when the test window closes. "Some time Tuesday" is not good enough.

## Done when
- Five campaigns are live, all launched within the same window.
- Five hypotheses are written down, dated before launch.
- The tracker holds launch timestamps and the do-not-touch-until date.
- A real conversion event has been observed after launch, not just impressions.

## Output
- `Campaign Tracker.md` — one row per campaign: name, hypothesis, launch time,
  budget, status
- `hypotheses/<campaign>.md` if the hypothesis needs more than a line

## Notes
The urge to optimise on day two is the single most expensive habit in paid social. The
tracker's do-not-touch date exists to make that urge visible rather than persuasive.
